What We Cover

Life coverage,
explained in plain English

Every policy type exists for a different reason. Here's a closer look at the core pieces of life insurance, and where each one tends to fit.

Term Life

Term Life Insurance

Straightforward, budget-friendly protection for a set period of time — often while you're raising kids, paying off a mortgage, or building toward retirement.

  • Level premiums and a level death benefit for 10, 15, 20, or 30-year terms
  • The most affordable way to secure a large death benefit while your family's needs are highest
  • Many policies are convertible to permanent coverage later without new medical underwriting
  • We shop multiple A-rated carriers to find the best rate for your health class
Get started →
Whole Life

Whole Life Insurance

Permanent, lifelong coverage with a guaranteed death benefit and a cash value component that grows at a guaranteed rate — a policy that never expires as long as premiums are paid.

  • Coverage that lasts your entire life, with premiums that never increase
  • Guaranteed cash value growth you can borrow against for emergencies or opportunities
  • Potential for dividends with participating policies, depending on carrier
  • A common tool for estate planning, legacy gifts, and final expense certainty
Get started →
Universal Life

Universal Life Insurance

Permanent coverage with more flexibility than whole life — adjust your premium payments and death benefit over time as your income and priorities change.

  • Flexible premiums and adjustable death benefits within policy limits
  • Cash value that grows based on current interest rates or, with indexed universal life, a market index
  • Options to fine-tune coverage as your mortgage, income, or family situation evolves
  • A fit for those who want permanent coverage with room to adapt
Get started →
Final Expense

Final Expense & Burial Insurance

A smaller, simplified whole life policy designed to cover funeral costs, medical bills, and other end-of-life expenses so your family isn't left to cover them out of pocket.

  • Smaller death benefits, typically $5,000–$50,000, sized to cover final expenses
  • Simplified issue underwriting — often no medical exam required
  • Fixed premiums and a guaranteed payout that never expires
  • A practical option for seniors or anyone who wants coverage in place quickly
Get started →
Disability Income

Disability Income Insurance

Your paycheck is often your biggest asset — disability income insurance replaces a portion of it if an illness or injury keeps you from working, protecting your family's finances when you can't.

  • Replaces a portion of your income, typically 50–65%, if you're unable to work due to a covered illness or injury
  • Short-term policies cover weeks to months; long-term policies can pay benefits for years or until retirement age
  • Own-occupation policies pay out even if you're able to work in a different line of work
  • Especially important for self-employed individuals and business owners without an employer-sponsored plan
